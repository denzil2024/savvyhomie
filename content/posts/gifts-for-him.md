## Questions People Ask

<details open>
<summary>What if I don't know his exact taste?</summary>
<p>Stick to categories built around daily use instead of personal style: food and snacks, grooming basics, or a home bar pick work for almost any guy. Save picks from Hobbies or the Personalized Keepsakes section for someone whose specific interests are already known.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>How do I narrow down nine categories to the right one?</summary>
<p>Match the category to how he spends a normal week, not a hobby he mentioned once at a barbecue. Someone who grills every weekend needs a different starting point than someone rebuilding a desk setup, and most people fit clearly into two or three of these categories, not all nine.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>What's a safe gift if he says he doesn't want anything?</summary>
<p>Consumables are the safest option here, coffee, hot sauce, jerky, spice rubs. They get used up instead of sitting around as one more thing to find space for, which is usually what's behind "I don't need anything" in the first place.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>How much should I spend?</summary>
<p>This list runs from under $20 (jerky, hot sauce, playing cards) to over $150 (the duffel bag, the dumbbell set), so budget doesn't limit the options much. Let the occasion set the range instead, an office gift exchange calls for a different pick than a gift for a spouse.</p>
</details>

## Final Thoughts

The best gift here depends on which category matches him, not which pick ranks highest on this list. Someone who grills every weekend and someone who reads on the couch every night need completely different picks, and that's fine. Pick two or three from the categories that fit him well rather than trying to cover every base at once.
