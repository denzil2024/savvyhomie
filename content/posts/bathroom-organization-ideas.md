## FAQ

<details><summary>Where should I start organizing a small bathroom?</summary>Start with whichever spot causes the biggest daily friction, most commonly the under-sink cabinet or a drawer that won't close. Fixing the zone touched every single day shows the biggest difference fastest.</details>

<details><summary>How do I organize a bathroom with no linen closet?</summary>Use the wall above the toilet, a rolling cart, or an over-the-door rack to store towels and backup supplies instead of relying on built-in storage that isn't there. A ladder rack or freestanding tower works well in a bathroom corner too.</details>

<details><summary>What's the best way to organize a shared medicine cabinet?</summary>Split the shelf by person or category using labeled bins, since a shared cabinet without divisions turns into one mixed pile fast. Clear organizers also make it easy to spot expired bottles without unloading the whole shelf.</details>

<details><summary>How do I keep bathroom drawers from turning into a junk drawer?</summary>Use dividers sized for what's stored inside, small trays for bobby pins and clips, bottle slots for anything that rolls. A drawer with no divisions will always slide back into one pile no matter how it starts out.</details>

<details><summary>What should go on the counter versus stay in a cabinet?</summary>Keep only what's used daily on the counter, grouped on a tray so it reads as intentional instead of cluttered. Everything else, backups and occasional-use items, belongs behind a door where it's out of the way but still reachable.</details>

<details><summary>How do you store hair tools safely in a small bathroom?</summary>A wall-mounted organizer or a heat-resistant mat with a cooling pocket keeps hot tools off the counter and their cords untangled. Both solve the two real problems at once, safe heat storage and cord management.</details>

## Final Thoughts

None of these 44 ideas fix a whole bathroom by themselves, and trying all of them in one weekend means finishing none of them well. Pick the zone that's bothering you right now more than any other, the drawer that won't close, the counter that's always cluttered, and start there. A bathroom organized one zone at a time still ends up more usable than one attempted all at once and abandoned halfway through.
