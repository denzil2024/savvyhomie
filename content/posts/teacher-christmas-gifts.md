## Questions People Ask

<details open>
<summary>How much should I spend on a teacher Christmas gift?</summary>
<p>Most individual teacher gifts land between $15 and $30. For a shared class gift split among many families, the total can go higher since each family's share stays small, a $50 to $75 combined gift is common for a full classroom.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>Is a gift card better than a wrapped gift?</summary>
<p>A gift card is never a bad choice, most teachers say they'd rather have one than another item to find space for. It only reads as impersonal if it's the only thing on the tag, pairing it with something small and specific, like the candle set or the hot cocoa set above, closes that gap.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>Should a whole class give one shared gift instead of everyone giving separately?</summary>
<p>It's often the better option. One well-chosen gift, like the wall calendar or the gift basket above, avoids a teacher ending up with ten of the same candle. If the class is doing a group gift, small individual touches like a card or a drawing from each kid still land well alongside it.</p>
</details>

<summary>What if I don't know the teacher's personal taste yet?</summary>
<p>Stick to broadly useful picks over anything personalized, a candle set, socks, or a snack basket work regardless of taste, while a monogrammed item or a specific scent is a bigger gamble early in the year. Save the more personal picks for a teacher you or your child has known for a while.</p>

## Final Thoughts

A good teacher gift doesn't need a theme or a big reveal, it just needs to skip the drawer where the extra mugs go. Pick something from coffee and drinkware or desk essentials if you don't know her well yet, and save the personalized picks for a teacher you've had for more than one year.
