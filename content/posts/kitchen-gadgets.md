## Questions People Ask

<details open>
<summary>What kitchen gadgets are worth buying?</summary>
<p>The ones that solve a specific, recurring problem, not a one-time party trick. Look for tools that save real time on something already cooked weekly, like a garlic press or a good chef's knife, rather than a single-use gadget bought for one recipe and forgotten after.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>How do I know a gadget won't just end up in a drawer?</summary>
<p>Picture the last time the problem it solves came up. If it was within the last week or two, the gadget probably earns its space. If it's been months since a mandoline slicer or a fish spatula would have helped, it's likely to sit unused no matter how useful it looks in a product photo.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>What kitchen gadgets should someone starting a kitchen from scratch buy first?</summary>
<p>A sharp chef's knife, a cutting board, a set of measuring tools, and a couple of good spatulas cover most everyday cooking. Specialty tools like a fish spatula or a splatter screen can wait until a specific need for them shows up in the kitchen.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>Are more expensive kitchen gadgets worth the extra cost?</summary>
<p>Sometimes, but not automatically. A cast iron skillet or a solid chef's knife tends to justify a higher price with decades of use. A gadget reached for only a few times a year rarely needs the pricier version. The basic one usually does the same job.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>How many kitchen gadgets does one kitchen need?</summary>
<p>There's no fixed number. It depends on how often someone cooks and what they cook most. A kitchen used daily for real meals benefits from more specialized tools than one used mostly for reheating and quick snacks.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>What's the difference between a kitchen gadget and a kitchen tool?</summary>
<p>Not much beyond framing. "Tool" tends to imply something that's proven itself over time, while "gadget" can carry a slightly gimmicky connotation. The best kitchen gadgets earn their way into being called tools once they've been used regularly enough to matter.</p>
</details>

## Final Thoughts

The best kitchen gadgets never feel like gadgets after a few weeks of owning them. They just quietly become the thing reached for without thinking, the peeler that finally glides, the scale that stops a recipe from guessing, the bench scraper that shows up in almost every meal. Start with whichever category matches the biggest daily annoyance in the kitchen right now, and let the rest fill in over time as a real need for them shows up.
