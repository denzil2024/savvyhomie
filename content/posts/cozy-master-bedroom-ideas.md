I'll be honest. Bedroom advice out there treats every room the same. A guest room gets the same tips as the room you share every night, and that's never sat right with me. Your primary bedroom has to serve more purposes than a guest room ever will, yet it still gets decorated like an afterthought. That's backwards.

It's where you watch TV, read, unwind, and spend more private time than anywhere else in the house. No wonder standard decor advice falls short here. So instead of more bedding and throw pillows, this list focuses on what earns its place, real seating, a TV setup that doesn't take over the wall, matched nightstands, proper light control, and rugs sized for the room. ## FAQ

<details>
<summary>What size rug do you need for a king bed?</summary>
9x12 is the real standard starting point for a king bed, sized so it runs at least 18 to 24 inches past the sides and foot of the bed. Anything smaller leaves bare, cold floor showing right where your feet land when you get out of bed.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Is having a TV in the bedroom bad for sleep?</summary>
The blue light and the noise both work against sleep, and watching TV in bed specifically trains the brain to treat the bed as an awake space, not a sleep one. A low console or wall mount away from the headboard, plus turning it off well before bedtime, meaningfully cuts that effect without giving up the TV entirely.
</details>

<details>
<summary>How high should a TV be mounted in a bedroom?</summary>
Somewhere around 48 to 60 inches off the floor works for a typical setup, higher than a living room mount because the viewing angle is from lying down or propped against pillows, not sitting upright. The real test is whether your neck has to tilt back to see the screen. That's the sign it's mounted too low.
</details>

<details>
<summary>How do you stop a rug from sliding on a hardwood floor?</summary>
A grip pad cut to size underneath does it, holding everything flat against the floor rather than letting it slide or bunch at the edges with regular walking. It also makes the whole thing feel thicker underfoot.
</details>

<details>
<summary>How far should a curtain rod extend past the window?</summary>
The common range is 3 to 6 inches past the frame on each side for a standard window, more for a larger one. That gap is what lets the curtains pull fully open without covering any of the glass.
</details>

<details>
<summary>What size nightstands work best for a king bed?</summary>
Look for a tabletop close to level with the mattress, within a few inches either way, and enough width, 24 to 30 inches is the common range, so it doesn't look undersized next to a king-size bed. Matching height matters more than matching style for how balanced the room feels.
</details>

## Final Thoughts

A primary bedroom carries more weight than any other bedroom in the house. Still most are furnished like every other bedroom with a bed two nightstands and little else.

The fixes that matter here are not more bedding or another throw pillow. A shared full-sized room needs somewhere to sit a TV that does not dominate the wall matching nightstands and lighting that can be adjusted instead of simply turned off.

Start with whichever gap is most obvious the next time you stand in the room. The rest can wait.
