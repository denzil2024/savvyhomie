A small bathroom vanity gets treated like it only has one surface: the counter. In reality it's at least five separate zones stacked around that same sink, above the mirror, beside the cabinet, inside it, in the drawers, and on the counter itself. Most bathrooms are only using one of them.

These 33 small bathroom vanity storage ideas work through every one of those zones on purpose, so a sink with no floor room left still ends up with real storage instead of a counter buried under everything that didn't fit anywhere else.

The [Bathroom Organization Ideas](/blog/bathroom-organization-ideas/) guide covers the rest of the room, and the [Over the Toilet Storage Ideas](/blog/over-the-toilet-storage-ideas/), [Toilet Paper Storage Ideas](/blog/diy-toilet-paper-storage/), [Toilet Paper Holder Ideas](/blog/diy-toilet-paper-holder/), and [Under Sink Organizer Ideas](/blog/diy-under-sink-organizer-bathroom/) guides cover storage near the toilet and under the sink specifically.

## FAQ

<details><summary>How do you add storage to a small bathroom vanity with no extra floor room?</summary>Look up and sideways before assuming there's nowhere left to go. A medicine cabinet claims the wall above the mirror, a narrow rolling cart fits a gap too tight for a fixed cabinet, and a slim shelf between the counter and the mirror uses a strip of wall that's usually bare.</details>

<details><summary>What's the best way to organize a small bathroom vanity drawer?</summary>Adjustable divider panels or stackable shallow trays both split one undivided drawer into defined sections, which matters more in a small vanity where a single drawer often has to hold several categories at once. Pick dividers for a shallow drawer and stacked trays for a deep one.</details>

<details><summary>How do I fit storage around the pipes under a pedestal sink?</summary>A cabinet built with the plumbing cutout worked in from the start reclaims that footprint instead of giving up on it entirely, which is usually the only real option once a pedestal or wall-mount sink exposes the pipe. A narrow rolling cart beside the sink is the other real workaround when a fitted cabinet isn't possible.</details>

<details><summary>Are floating shelves a good idea for a small bathroom vanity?</summary>Yes, especially under a floating vanity where the gap below it is otherwise wasted, and above the sink where a slim shelf between the counter and mirror uses a strip of wall nothing else claims. Pair open shelves with a few baskets if the actual contents look messier than the shelf itself.</details>

<details><summary>How do I keep a small vanity counter from looking cluttered?</summary>Group what's already there onto one tray instead of leaving bottles scattered, and move anything used less than daily into a cabinet or drawer. A matching dispenser set also does real work here, since mismatched original packaging is often what makes a counter read as messy in the first place.</details>

## Final Thoughts

A small bathroom vanity isn't small so much as underused. Once the wall above the mirror, the gap beside the cabinet, and the inside of every drawer are all pulling their weight, the one counter that used to hold everything finally gets to hold almost nothing.
