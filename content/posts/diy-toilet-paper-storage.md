Toilet paper storage that doesn't involve a drill tends to get treated as one single category, when it's four or five different approaches that solve different problems. A freestanding stand works where a wall mount can't, and a woven basket solves a completely different issue than a stacking tower does.

These 25 toilet paper storage ideas are sorted by how each one works (freestanding, no-drill wall mounts, enclosed holders, baskets, shelving, and towers), so you can match the method to your wall, your floor space, and your lease before picking a style.

The [Bathroom Organization Ideas](/blog/bathroom-organization-ideas/) guide covers the rest of the room, and the [Over the Toilet Storage Ideas](/blog/over-the-toilet-storage-ideas/) guide covers the space above the tank specifically.

## FAQ

<details><summary>What's the best toilet paper storage for a rental?</summary>Anything that skips the wall entirely or uses adhesive or suction instead of screws, a freestanding stand, an adhesive-mounted holder, or a suction cup holder. All three leave the wall exactly as it was on move-out day.</details>

<details><summary>How do you store toilet paper without drilling into the wall?</summary>A freestanding stand needs no wall contact at all, and adhesive or suction mounts hold real weight without screws or anchors. A recessed holder is the one option here that does require cutting into the wall, so it's worth knowing that going in.</details>

<details><summary>What's the best way to store backup toilet paper rolls?</summary>A tall basket, a stacking tower, or a stand with a built-in storage compartment all keep several rolls upright and contained in one spot. Loose rolls on a shelf or the floor tend to slide around and end up scattered.</details>

<details><summary>Do freestanding toilet paper stands tip over easily?</summary>A stand with a narrow, lightweight base can wobble when a roll gets pulled, but a weighted base or a wider footprint keeps it steady through normal daily use. Check the base width before buying if the bathroom sees heavy daily traffic.</details>

<details><summary>Can toilet paper storage fit in a very tight bathroom?</summary>Yes. A slim freestanding pole, a narrow vertical tower, or a wall-mounted basket all use a fraction of the floor or wall space a wider cabinet or shelf would need. Measure the actual gap before choosing between a floor stand and a wall mount.</details>

## Final Thoughts

Toilet paper storage without a drill comes down to matching the method to what the bathroom allows (floor space for a stand, a wall that can take adhesive or suction, or neither if the room is tight enough to need a slim vertical tower instead). Once the method is settled, the style (basket, tower, or enclosed holder) is the easy part.
