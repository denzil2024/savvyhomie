The cabinet under a bathroom sink usually holds exactly one system: pile everything in and dig when you need something. It's also the most awkward storage spot in the house. A pipe runs through the middle, the door opens onto nothing, and half the floor is invisible without kneeling down to check it.

The 30 ideas below tackle that cabinet from six real angles. If layout is the fight, start with the door, a sliding system, or the plumbing's own clearance. If the real problem is volume, skip straight to small-item sorting, everyday bins, or bulk backstock instead.

The [Bathroom Organization Ideas](/blog/bathroom-organization-ideas/) guide covers the rest of the room, and the [Over the Toilet Storage Ideas](/blog/over-the-toilet-storage-ideas/), [Toilet Paper Storage Ideas](/blog/diy-toilet-paper-storage/), and [Toilet Paper Holder Ideas](/blog/diy-toilet-paper-holder/) guides cover storage and fixtures near the toilet specifically.

## FAQ

<details><summary>How do you organize under a bathroom sink with pipes in the way?</summary>Treat each side of the pipe as its own zone rather than fighting the plumbing for one continuous shelf. A two-tier sliding organizer split into separate baskets on either side of the pipe works for that, and a welded pipe-fitting frame handles it too if your plumbing runs at an odd angle.</details>

<details><summary>What should you store under the bathroom sink?</summary>Backup toiletries, cleaning supplies, spare rolls, first aid basics, and hair accessories all make sense down there. Anything used daily is better off in a door-mounted caddy or on the door itself, so it doesn't get buried behind the backstock.</details>

<details><summary>Are under-sink organizers renter-friendly?</summary>Most are. Sliding baskets, stackable shelves, and pipe-fitting frames all sit loose in the cabinet with no drilling required, so they come out and move with you the same way they went in.</details>

<details><summary>Is metal safe to use under a bathroom sink, or will it rust?</summary>Look for a rust-resistant or powder-coated finish, since the area under a sink stays more humid than the rest of the bathroom. A rubber cabinet liner underneath any metal rack also helps keep condensation off the cabinet floor itself.</details>

<details><summary>What's the most common mistake when buying an under-sink organizer?</summary>Measuring only the width and skipping depth and pipe placement. A shelf that technically fits the opening can still hit the drain pipe or stick out past the door, so measure all three before ordering anything with a rigid frame.</details>

## Final Thoughts

An under sink cabinet doesn't need more room. It needs the room it already has put to use. Between the door, the shelves, and the floor, these ideas take the same square footage and just stop wasting it.
