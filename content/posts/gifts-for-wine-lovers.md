## Questions People Ask

<details open>
<summary>What if they're serious about wine and I'm not?</summary>
<p>Stick to tools that work regardless of expertise, an aerator, a preservation stopper, a cheese board, rather than trying to pick a specific bottle or region you don't know well. A good tool gets used no matter what they eventually pour into it.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>Should I get a gadget or an actual bottle of wine?</summary>
<p>A tool or accessory usually lands better than picking a bottle for them, because wine taste is personal enough that a wrong bottle just sits unopened. Something useful gets reached for regardless of which wine ends up in the glass.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>What do I get someone who already owns every gadget?</summary>
<p>Look toward the learning and experience side of the list instead of another tool. A tasting kit, a guided journal, or a blind-tasting game gives them something to do with wine rather than one more thing to find shelf space for.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>Is it weird to give a non-alcohol gift to someone who takes wine seriously?</summary>
<p>Not at all. Glassware, storage, journals, and tasting kits are things a serious wine person wants but rarely buys for themselves, because spending on accessories can feel like an unnecessary splurge compared to spending on the wine itself.</p>
</details>

## Final Thoughts

A good wine gift doesn't have to be another bottle. Pick the tool or accessory that solves an annoyance they already have, and it'll get used long after this gift-giving season ends.
