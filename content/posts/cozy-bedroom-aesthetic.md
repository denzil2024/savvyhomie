## FAQ

<details>
<summary>How much weight can a hanging chair hold?</summary>
It depends entirely on the mount, not the chair itself, a rated ceiling hook or stand typically holds 250 to 350 pounds. Always check the hardware's rating specifically, since a chair's own weight limit means nothing without proper mounting.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Is a desk in the bedroom bad for sleep?</summary>
Not if it's positioned out of direct sightline from the bed, tucked in a corner rather than facing it. The bigger factor is screen use right before bed, not the desk's presence in the room.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Do room dividers need to be permanent?</summary>
No, nearly all of the options here are freestanding or fold flat, meaning nothing gets installed into the wall or ceiling. A rented bedroom can use almost any of them without losing a security deposit.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Can a hanging chair go in an apartment with low ceilings?</summary>
A freestanding stand works better than a ceiling mount in that case, since it doesn't depend on ceiling height at all. Measure the stand's total height against the room before buying, not just the chair itself.
</details>

<details>
<summary>What's the easiest way to add a desk without shrinking the room?</summary>
A corner desk or a fold-down wall-mounted option both use less room than a full rectangular desk would. Either keeps the center of the room open instead of splitting it in two.
</details>

## Final Thoughts

A bedroom aesthetic search mostly answers what color to paint and which lights to string up. It rarely answers what happens when the room also needs to be a workspace, seat a second person, or split in half for privacy.

Start with whichever gap shows up first in daily use, not the one that looks best on a mood board. A desk corner solves a real problem every weekday. A divider only matters on the days the room needs to be two rooms. Match the fix to how the room gets used day to day, and the rest can wait.
