Gifts for neighbors can be hard to get right: go too big and it feels like you're trying too hard, keep it too small and it reads like an afterthought. The 27 picks below sit in that middle ground, organized by porch decor, pantry treats, cozy touches, kitchen finds, and small extras, so it's easy to grab one or two without overthinking the whole list.

Buying for more than one person this year? The [45 Christmas Gift Ideas](/blog/christmas-gift-ideas/) guide covers everyone else on the list, and the [27 Christmas Gifts for Coworkers](/blog/christmas-gifts-for-coworkers/) guide is worth a look if there's a work exchange to plan for too.

## Questions People Ask

<details open>
<summary>How much should I spend on a gift for a neighbor?</summary>
<p>Most neighbor gifts land between $10 and $25, more like a kind gesture than an obligation. Pantry items and small extras, like the popcorn tin or the ornament set above, sit comfortably in that range without looking cheap.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>Do I need to get something for every neighbor on the street?</summary>
<p>Not every neighbor needs a gift, just the ones you interact with regularly: next-door neighbors, anyone who's helped with a package or a pet, or someone you talk to often. A wave-and-smile neighbor a few houses down doesn't need to be on the list.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>What if I don't know about a neighbor's food allergies?</summary>
<p>When in doubt, skip the food and go with something like the doormat, the diffuser, or the beanie instead. If a food gift feels right anyway, the nut tray and the cheese board are the two items on this list worth double-checking before giving.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>When's the best time to drop off a neighbor gift?</summary>
<p>Anytime in the two weeks before Christmas works, early enough that it doesn't feel rushed, but not so early it gets lost among other holiday deliveries. Avoid Christmas Day itself, most people are busy with their own plans by then.</p>
</details>

## Final Thoughts

A good neighbor gift doesn't need to say much: it just needs to show up. Pick something useful over something elaborate, and the rest takes care of itself.
