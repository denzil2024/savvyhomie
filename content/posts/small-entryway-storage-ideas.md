Good small entryway storage starts with a room that has to work before anyone's even taken a coat off. A pile of shoes by the door does more to make a house look chaotic than a messy room down the hall ever could, mostly because it's the first thing anyone sees.

The real problem comes down to width more than anything else. An entryway this narrow rarely has room for a full closet, so shoes, coats, and whatever's in a hand on the way in all end up sharing a footprint smaller than any other room in the house.

Working on more than just the entryway? [Small Space Organization Ideas](/blog/small-space-organization-ideas/) covers every other room that ran out of room too.

## FAQ

<details>
<summary>How do you organize a small entryway with no closet?</summary>
Start with a hall tree or a shoe cabinet with hooks built in, since that covers shoes and coats in one piece of furniture without needing a closet at all. Wall-mounted storage picks up whatever the furniture alone can't hold.
</details>

<details>
<summary>What furniture works best in a small entryway?</summary>
A slim console table, a narrow shoe cabinet, or a hall tree all work in a room too small for standard-depth furniture, since each one is built shallow on purpose. A bar cart or a narrow bookcase can stand in for any of them if there's no floor room for a dedicated piece at all.
</details>

<details>
<summary>What's the best way to store shoes in a narrow entryway?</summary>
Wall-mounted or vertical shoe storage works better than a floor rack in a narrow entryway, since it uses height instead of width. A slim tilt-out cabinet or a ladder-style rack holds a full shoe rotation in the same footprint as a single pair sitting on the floor.
</details>

<details>
<summary>How do you organize keys and mail by the door?</summary>
A shallow ledge shelf, a floating shelf with hooks, or a console table drawer all give keys and mail one fixed spot to go instead of the floor or a counter somewhere else in the house. Once there's a fixed spot, keys stop getting lost.
</details>

<details>
<summary>How do you store coats without a coat closet?</summary>
Wall-mounted hooks, a peg rail, or a hall tree all replace a coat closet without needing to build one. Adjustable or grid-mounted hooks work best for a household with a mix of bulky coats and thin jackets.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Can a bar cart work as entryway storage?</summary>
Yes, especially in an entryway too small to spare floor room for furniture that only does one job. Its open tiers catch keys, mail, and sunglasses the same way a console table would, and it rolls away the day it's needed somewhere else.
</details>

## Final Thoughts

An entryway rarely fails from lack of room. It fails because shoes, coats, and the loose stuff that ends up in a hand on the way in all compete for the same small spot.

Fix whatever's piling up first, not the whole entryway at once. A floor full of shoes points straight at the shoe furniture. Coats slung over a chair point at the wall hooks or the hall tree instead.
