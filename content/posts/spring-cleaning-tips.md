## FAQ

<details><summary>How do you do a proper spring cleaning?</summary>Start high and work your way down, since dust from a ceiling fan or a shelf always ends up on the floor eventually. Dust first, then move to the floors, then finish with the surfaces that fight back hardest, like grout and a stovetop that's seen a whole winter of cooking. Doing it in the wrong order just means cleaning the same spot twice.</details>

<details><summary>What are the top 10 tips for spring cleaning?</summary>Reach for an extendable pole on ceiling fan blades instead of a wobbly chair, and claw dust out of blinds slat by slat instead of giving them one flat swipe that barely touches the front edge. On floors, a steam mop beats dragging out a dirty mop bucket, and a drill-mounted brush clears grout in a fraction of the time a toothbrush would take. Squeegee your shower glass after every shower so soap scum never gets the chance to build up, and vacuum the mattress itself, not just the sheets on top of it. Wear gloves before the first spray bottle comes out, kneel on a cushion for any low scrubbing, keep a labeled bin so cleaning cloths stay sorted by room, and sanitize light switches and phone screens, since both get touched constantly and almost never get cleaned.</details>

<details><summary>What is the 5 5 5 rule for cleaning?</summary>It's a decluttering method, not a spring cleaning one: pick 5 items to throw away, 5 to give away, and 5 to put back where they belong, repeated room by room. It pairs well with a spring clean since clearing clutter first makes the actual cleaning faster.</details>

<details><summary>What should I use for spring cleaning?</summary>A mix of tools and solutions, not just one all-purpose spray. A steam mop or wet-dry vacuum mop for floors, a paste cleaner for baked-on grease, a vinegar-based glass cleaner, a disinfecting spray for counters, and real protective gear like gloves and a kneeling pad for the parts of the job that are hardest on your body.</details>

<details><summary>What are the 7 stages of cleaning?</summary>A professional routine typically breaks it into preparation, dusting, high surfaces, low surfaces, floors, bathrooms and kitchens, and a final walkthrough. Dust always comes before floors in a real sequence, since cleaning a floor before dusting the shelves above it just means doing the floor twice.</details>

<details><summary>What are 10 cleaning materials commonly used in housekeeping?</summary>Microfiber cloths, an all-purpose or paste cleaner, a disinfecting spray, a glass cleaner, rubber gloves, a scrub brush or sponge, a vacuum, a mop and bucket, a squeegee, and a carpet or upholstery cleaner cover the bulk of what a real housekeeping routine needs.</details>

<details><summary>What are some effective cleaning tips and hacks?</summary>Let a steam cleaner loosen old shelf-paper glue instead of scraping it by hand, use a rubber broom to pull embedded pet hair out of carpet before vacuuming, and spray a stain the moment it happens instead of scrubbing it in with a dry rag. Working top to bottom and dry to wet in each room also saves redoing a surface twice.</details>

<details><summary>What should I include in my spring cleaning checklist?</summary>Every room, not just the visible ones: ceiling fans and blinds, baseboards, grout and tile, the mattress and bedding, high-touch spots like light switches and phone screens, and the cleaning solutions and protective gear needed to get through all of it in one pass.</details>

<details><summary>What is spring cleaning in housekeeping?</summary>A yearly deep reset that goes after everything a regular weekly routine skips: the ceiling fan blade, the grout line, the mattress fabric itself, not just the surfaces that get wiped down every week.</details>

<details><summary>What is the difference between deep cleaning and spring cleaning?</summary>Deep cleaning can happen any time and often targets one room or problem area. Spring cleaning is the whole-house version done once a year, covering every room in the same pass rather than tackling trouble spots as they come up.</details>

<details><summary>What is the 5 step cleaning process?</summary>Clear clutter, dust, scrub the surfaces that need it, mop or vacuum the floors, then sanitize high-touch spots last so nothing gets re-contaminated by an earlier step. Skipping the order, like mopping before dusting, usually means redoing a step.</details>

<details><summary>What is the purpose of spring cleaning?</summary>It exists to catch everything that builds up too slowly to notice week to week: the dust behind fan blades, the grime worked into grout, the odor that settles into a mattress or a rug. None of that shows up in a quick weekly routine, so a once-a-year deep pass is the only thing built to catch it.</details>

## Final Thoughts

You don't have to do all 40 of these in one weekend. Pick one category, work through it, and move to the next whenever you're ready. Spring cleaning is the one pass each year that reaches past what your weekly wipe-down ever touches, the fan blade, the grout, the mattress, the corner behind the stairs, and by the time you've worked through the list, the whole place feels like yours again, not just picked up.
