Mama, if you've ever shoved a basket of mail behind the couch thirty seconds before someone rang the doorbell, you already know exactly why this list exists. Cords tangle behind the TV. A closet rod runs out of room. The junk drawer stops closing all the way. None of it's a big deal by itself, it's just a lot of small annoying things happening in the same house at the same time.

These 33 tips for decluttering your home work through the real trouble spots in order: cords and cables, closets, kitchen drawers, dead space around doors and corners, the paper pile that never shrinks, and the small stuff that clutters a counter.

The [Home Organization Ideas](/blog/home-organization-ideas/) guide covers the full house room by room, and the [House Organization Tips](/blog/house-organization-tips/) guide covers the clear-sort-contain-label process behind any of it.

## FAQ

<details><summary>What is the 3-3-3 rule for decluttering?</summary>Pick three items to throw away, three to donate, and three to put back where they belong, all in one short session instead of one marathon purge. It works because three of each feels doable on a Tuesday night, which is exactly why it gets finished when a whole-room overhaul doesn't.</details>

<details><summary>What is the 20/20/20 rule for decluttering?</summary>If you can replace an item for under 20 dollars in under 20 minutes, it's safe to let go of, even if there's a small chance you'll want it again someday. It's a permission-to-toss rule more than an organizing one, aimed at the stuff people hang onto out of guilt rather than actual need.</details>

<details><summary>In what order should I declutter my house?</summary>Cords and cables first, since a cable sleeve or a cord box fixes the most visible mess in minutes. From there: closets, then kitchen drawers, then paper, then whatever small stuff is cluttering the counter. It's roughly the order this list follows, and each fix builds momentum for the next.</details>

<details><summary>What are 10 tips for decluttering my home quickly?</summary>Cords and cables go fastest: a cable sleeve, a cord box, adhesive clips, a cable winder, and a cord cover channel all take minutes apiece and clear the most visible mess in a room. After that, a wall-mounted mail sorter, a shredder for old statements, a junk drawer tray, a charging caddy, and a battery case knock out the next five fastest wins.</details>

<details><summary>What should I remove first when decluttering?</summary>Whatever's creating the most visible chaos in the room you're standing in right now, loose cords or a mail pile, not whatever's theoretically the biggest project somewhere else in the house. Tackling the eyesore first builds the motivation to keep going into the harder spots.</details>

<details><summary>What are 12 items I should throw away before moving?</summary>Expired pantry items, old medications, dead batteries, single socks, chargers for devices you no longer own, duplicate kitchen tools, worn-out towels, broken hangers, expired coupons, old phone cases, takeout menus for closed restaurants, and anything still in its box from a gift two years ago. None of it's worth the box space or the unpacking time on the other end.</details>

<details><summary>What not to throw away when decluttering?</summary>Anything with real resale value, important documents like birth certificates and titles, sentimental items you'd regret losing a month later, and tools or supplies you use less than monthly but would cost real money to replace. The goal is removing what's sitting unused, not emptying every drawer on principle.</details>

<details><summary>Which room should I declutter first?</summary>Whichever one bothers you the most every single day, usually the kitchen or the entryway, since that's where the payoff gets noticed immediately. A guest room nobody uses can wait; the counter you see every morning can't.</details>

<details><summary>What are common decluttering mistakes?</summary>Buying bins before sorting is the big one, since a matching bin set just moves an unsorted mess into a nicer container. Right behind it: trying to do the whole house in one weekend instead of one area at a time, and keeping things "just in case" without a real plan for when that case might come.</details>

## Final Thoughts

None of these 33 tips require clearing out a whole weekend. Pick the one area bothering you most right now, whether that's the cord tangle, the closet rod, or the mail pile, and fix that single spot before moving to the next.
