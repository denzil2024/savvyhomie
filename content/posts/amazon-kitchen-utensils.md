## Questions People Ask

<details open>
<summary>Should I buy a full utensil set or individual pieces?</summary>
<p>A full set makes sense when starting a kitchen from scratch or replacing everything at once. Buying individual pieces makes more sense when most of the drawer is already covered and only one or two tools are missing.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>Is silicone or wood better for nonstick pans?</summary>
<p>Both protect a nonstick coating better than bare metal. Wood tends to hold up longer under high heat without any risk of melting, while silicone comes in more shapes and colors and is easier to clean in a dishwasher.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>Which category is the easiest place to start?</summary>
<p>Cooking and Serving, because it covers tools used almost every night, like tongs, a whisk, and a turner, rather than specialty pieces that only come out for specific dishes.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>Do the single-job tools get used often enough to be worth it?</summary>
<p>It depends on how often that specific job comes up. A salad spinner or a can opener earns its space fast in most kitchens, while something like the spider skimmer only makes sense for someone who fries or blanches food regularly.</p>
</details>

<summary>Do wood utensils need special care compared to silicone or steel?</summary>
<p>Mostly just hand washing instead of the dishwasher, and an occasional wipe with food-safe oil to keep the wood from drying out. Beyond that, they hold up about as well as any other material with regular use.</p>
</details>

## Final Thoughts

A full set solves the problem of an empty drawer. A single sharp buy fixes the one tool that's been letting you down. Figure out which one you're dealing with before adding anything to the cart, and the right pick gets a lot easier to spot.
